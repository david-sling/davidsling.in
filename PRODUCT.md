## Platform

## Stack

**Deploy target:** Vercel.

## Users

Ranked by priority:

1. **Developer peers.** Engineers and builders working with AI coding agents. They arrive from GitHub, X/LinkedIn, a blog post, or a Wave or Equanim link.
   - **Their job:** judge whether David's work and thinking are worth their time.
   - **Success:** they try Wave or Equanim, read a post, or follow him.
2. **Future clients and collaborators.** Founders and teams who might want to build something with David later.
   - **Their job:** judge his taste, range, and how far he takes a product on his own.
   - **Success:** they remember him and start a conversation when the time comes.
3. **Hiring evaluators.** Managers and founders sizing up David's seniority.
   - **Their job:** check his track record and level.
   - **Success:** his credibility is clear from the work and the role history.

David is **not currently looking** for roles or freelance work. The site is for presence and credibility. Its primary action is to get in touch or follow, not to hire.

## Product Purpose

davidsling.in is David Sling's personal site: his portfolio, project case studies, and blog. It is a ground-up rebuild "for the AI era".

It exists to show the products David has shipped recently, which he built largely with AI agents, and to show the judgement and craft behind them. Each featured project gets a detailed case-study page.

It succeeds when a peer leaves thinking "this person builds with agents and still has taste", then explores a project or a post.

## Positioning

**An engineer with a designer's eye.**

- David is a frontend lead who designs, and his craft and taste hold up at AI speed.
- The site itself is part of the proof: its own quality has to back up the claim.
- AI-assisted building is part of the story but not the headline. The claim is about taste and judgement in shipped software, not about AI.

## Product Principles

1. **The site is the proof.** Every claim about craft has to be visible in the site itself. When in doubt, show the work rather than describe it.
2. **Peers first.** Write and structure for builders who read closely and can spot hand-waving. Use real engineering detail, not buzzwords.
3. **Honest about AI.** Credit the agents plainly and show the workflow without hype. The point is judgement and taste, not "AI did it".
4. **Depth over breadth.** Three projects told well beat a grid of eleven. Each case study goes from problem to decisions, trade-offs, and outcome.
5. **No pitch.** David isn't selling his availability. The calls to action are invitations to explore, read, or say hi.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA.

- **Motion:** David wants a lot of microinteractions, so every motion needs a `prefers-reduced-motion` path with fewer and gentler effects.
- **Interaction:** everything must work by keyboard, with visible focus.
- **Text effects:** animated text (scramble, split, marquee) must stay readable by screen readers.
